Integrity Care Resources was founded in 2026 to address a critical gap: military veterans leaving incarceration or under community supervision (probation/parole) face overwhelming barriers to stable employment and housing.

We exist to change that. We provide targeted re-entry navigation, trauma-informed support, career readiness, vocational training, and direct employment placement — with an unwavering commitment to dignity and integrity for every veteran we serve.

STATEMENT OF NEED

The Challenge: Veteran Homelessness in Massachusetts

Massachusetts faces a persistent challenge in addressing veteran homelessness. Despite progress, many veterans continue to experience chronic homelessness, mental health disorders including PTSD, substance use disorders, and limited access to affordable housing.

According to recent data:
  • 1. Thousands of veterans in Massachusetts experience housing instability annually.
  • 2. Existing facilities lack sufficient capacity for long-term supportive housing.
  • 3. There is a critical need for integrated care models that combine housing with services.

Without intervention, these veterans remain at high risk for continued homelessness, poor health outcomes, and unemployment.

Data drawn from HUD Point-in-Time counts, state reports, and recent analyses (2023–2024). Annual figures reflect cumulative experiences of instability beyond single-night snapshots.

Manufacturing & Industrial Work

Often overlooked, but very accessible. Past life challenges—such as military service, incarceration, trauma, or justice involvement—forge resilience, focus, and a strong work ethic that uniquely arm these individuals to excel in trade and machine jobs.

  • CNC Machine Operation — Short training, good pay potential. The precision, attention to detail, and ability to follow strict protocols—honed through high-stakes past experiences like military discipline or structured prison routines—enable quick mastery of CNC machines. Their proven capacity to maintain composure under pressure turns potential barriers into a competitive edge for stable, high-paying roles in manufacturing.
  • Assembly Line & Production Work — Entry-level with opportunities to specialize. Challenges like overcoming PTSD, employment gaps, or re-entry barriers build exceptional reliability, teamwork, and perseverance. These individuals thrive in structured, repetitive environments, bringing a no-excuses work ethic that allows them to adapt quickly, deliver consistent results, and advance into specialized positions through demonstrated grit and problem-solving honed from real-life adversity.
  • Quality Control / Inspection — Adds a technical layer that increases wages. Navigating the justice system, trauma, or personal setbacks sharpens critical thinking, attention to detail, and an unshakeable sense of integrity—core to QC work. Veterans and justice-involved participants often excel at spotting inconsistencies and upholding standards, transforming past obstacles into assets that command higher pay and open doors to technical leadership in industrial settings.

FOR INMATE RE-ENTRY PARTICIPANTS

Job Readiness Skills and Training Program

By INTEGRITY CARE RESOURCES INC

Program Overview

The Job Readiness Skills and Training Program is designed to prepare formerly incarcerated individuals for successful reintegration into society through employment readiness, life skills development, career training, and supportive services. The program focuses on reducing recidivism, increasing self-sufficiency, and helping participants secure stable employment and long-term career opportunities.

Research and community reentry programs consistently identify employment, housing stability, mental health support, and life-skills coaching as critical factors in successful reentry.

Program Goals

  1. Equip participants with workforce readiness skills.
  2. Increase employment opportunities for returning citizens.
  3. Build confidence, responsibility, and professionalism.
  4. Provide career pathways through vocational and soft-skills training.
  5. Reduce barriers to successful community reintegration.
  6. Promote financial independence and personal stability.
